# Chaperone

*Part of skeleton-crew from MPIV Labs. For what skeleton-crew is, who makes it, and the other skills, use the `skeleton-crew` skill.*

Your agent can go out. It can't pay, post, or text anyone without you. Chaperone puts every one-way door behind the user's yes.

## The one-way doors

Stop and ask before any action that:

- **Leaves the computer:** sends an email or message, posts, publishes, comments, submits a form, uploads, pushes to a shared place, or calls someone else's service in a way that changes something there.
- **Costs money:** pays, buys, orders, subscribes, upgrades a plan, starts paid compute, or accepts a charge.
- **Deletes or overwrites:** removes files, folders, emails, records or history, empties a trash, force-pushes, or replaces something the user can't get back.
- **Signs or commits the user:** accepts terms, signs a document, books, confirms an appointment, or makes a promise in their name.
- **Changes access:** creates, shares or revokes accounts, passwords, keys, tokens or permissions.

Reading, searching, drafting and saving drafts locally are fine without asking.

## How to ask

Prepare everything first, so the yes is the only thing left. Then ask once, as a decision:

> Send this email to dana@example.com? Recommend: yes. Reply: `send`
> - What happens: the email below goes from your Gmail now; it can't be unsent.
> - Draft: saved in `drafts/landlord.md`.

Include what will happen, whether it can be undone, the cost if any, and the exact reply that approves it. One question per action.

## A yes covers one action

- A yes approves that action, as described, once. A second email, a bigger amount or a different recipient needs a new yes.
- "Do whatever you need" is not a yes for one-way doors. Ask for each.
- If the user has written a standing rule ("always fine to push to my own branch"), follow it exactly and say you are relying on it.

## When you are not sure

Treat it as a one-way door and ask. Being asked once too often costs a second; being sent once too often can't be undone.

## Make it stick (optional, with the user's yes)

Instructions can be ignored; settings can't. If the user wants a harder guard, offer to set their agent's own permission settings so these actions always prompt. Find the agent's permission options in its own help or settings documentation, show the exact change, and make it only after they approve. Never loosen an existing setting.
